# 16. Dark radiation and N_eff

## Purpose of this chapter

This chapter explains Dark radiation and N_eff as one entry point into the current Hubble tension landscape. The handbook does not propose a new model; it provides a bounded reading map for existing literature and observational probes.

The central thesis of this chapter is:

> Changing N_eff can affect early expansion and the sound horizon, but it is strongly constrained by the CMB and primordial physics.

## Background

N_eff represents the effective number of relativistic species in the early Universe. Extra light particles or dark radiation could change this value.

Increasing N_eff can change the early expansion rate and the sound horizon, thereby affecting inferred H0. But CMB damping, primordial nucleosynthesis, matter-radiation equality, and other constraints also respond.

## What the reader should learn

This route is conceptually simple, but N_eff cannot be adjusted freely. It must pass several early-universe constraints simultaneously.

This perspective is necessary for reading the Hubble tension as a network of observational routes and failure boundaries rather than as a simple question of moving H0 up or down.

## Reading cautions

The reader should not use a single dataset, single paper, or isolated parameter value to make a definitive claim. The concept in this chapter must be read together with observational routes, model assumptions, and statistical treatment.

## Key points

- N_eff tracks early relativistic energy density.
- It can affect H0, but constraints are strong.
- CMB and primordial physics must be considered together.
